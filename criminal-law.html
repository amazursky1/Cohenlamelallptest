Criminal Law

Focused representation for criminal charges in New Jersey.

Being charged with a criminal offense can affect far more than a single court date. A pending case may create concerns about employment, family responsibilities, reputation and the possibility of significant legal consequences. Cohen & Lamela represents clients in criminal matters and draws on extensive experience in both Superior Court and Municipal Court.

The firm's approach begins with a careful review of the charge, the available evidence and the circumstances that led to the arrest or complaint. The attorneys work to make sure clients understand what is happening, what the prosecution is alleging and what legal or factual issues may be important to the defense.

Criminal matters may involve

  • Assault and other person-related offenses
  • Theft and property-related allegations
  • Drug-possession and related charges
  • Juvenile matters
  • Municipal and Superior Court criminal proceedings
  • Cases involving multiple charges or related court matters

How the firm can help

  • Review complaints, charging documents, police reports and available evidence
  • Explain the court process and the client's immediate obligations
  • Identify legal, factual and procedural issues that may affect the defense
  • Prepare clients for court appearances, hearings and other proceedings
  • Advocate for the client through negotiations, motions and trial when appropriate

Why early legal guidance matters

Criminal cases can move quickly, and important decisions are often made before a case reaches trial. Court dates, release conditions, evidence issues and communications with law enforcement or prosecutors can all matter. Speaking with an attorney early gives the defense an opportunity to understand the case before avoidable decisions are made.

What to expect when you call

Be prepared to explain the charge, the date and location of the incident, whether you have already appeared in court and the date of any upcoming proceeding. If you have a complaint, summons, bail or release paperwork, police report or other court documents, have them available for review.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I bring to a criminal law consultation?

Bring any complaint, summons, charging document, release paperwork, police report or court notice you have received. A complete set of documents helps the attorney understand the case more quickly.

Should I discuss the case with law enforcement before speaking with a lawyer?

If you are unsure about your rights or what is being requested, speaking with an attorney first can help you understand the situation before making decisions about further communication.

What happens at the first court appearance?

The procedure varies depending on the charge and court. The attorney can explain the purpose of the appearance, what documents may be reviewed and what the next stages of the case may involve.

Does every criminal case go to trial?

No. Some cases may be resolved through motions, negotiations or other court procedures, while others proceed to trial. The appropriate approach depends on the facts and legal issues involved.
