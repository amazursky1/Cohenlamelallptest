Domestic Violence

Domestic violence matters can be urgent, emotional and highly sensitive. Prompt legal guidance can help clients understand the process and immediate next steps.

Domestic Violence

Careful representation in urgent and sensitive proceedings.

Domestic violence matters can develop quickly and often involve immediate court dates, restrictions on contact, family concerns and allegations with serious consequences. Cohen & Lamela provides representation in domestic violence proceedings and helps clients understand what a court order requires and how to prepare for the next stage of the case.

These matters demand careful attention to both the written allegations and the history surrounding the dispute. The firm works with clients to organize relevant information, understand the court process and prepare for hearings in a focused and respectful manner.

Domestic violence representation may involve

  • Temporary and final restraining-order proceedings
  • Review of complaints, orders and hearing notices
  • Preparation for testimony and court appearances
  • Evidence such as messages, photographs, records or witness information
  • Questions concerning the requirements of an existing court order
  • Related legal issues that may arise from the same underlying events

How the firm can help

  • Review the allegations and all existing court orders
  • Explain the purpose and procedure of the upcoming hearing
  • Help organize documents, communications and other potentially relevant evidence
  • Prepare the client for court and the issues likely to be addressed
  • Provide courtroom representation and practical guidance throughout the matter

Do not ignore the terms of a court order

If a temporary or other court order has already been entered, read it carefully and follow its terms. Questions about what an order permits or prohibits should be addressed promptly. Bring the complete order and complaint to the first conversation so the attorney can review the exact language rather than relying only on a summary of what occurred.

What to expect when you call

The attorney will want to know whether an order has already been entered, the date of any hearing and the basic history of the events described in the complaint. If you have messages, photographs, police paperwork, prior orders or other documents that may be relevant, gather them for review.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if a temporary restraining order has already been entered?

Read the order carefully and follow its terms. Bring the complete order and complaint to the attorney so the exact restrictions and hearing information can be reviewed.

What should I bring to a consultation?

Bring all court orders, complaints, hearing notices, police paperwork and any relevant communications, photographs or other documentation you believe may relate to the allegations.

How quickly do these cases move?

Domestic violence proceedings can move quickly. If a hearing date has already been scheduled, contacting counsel promptly gives more time to review the allegations and prepare.

Can text messages or other communications be relevant?

They can be, depending on the facts. Preserve relevant communications rather than deleting or altering them, and allow the attorney to determine what may be useful.
