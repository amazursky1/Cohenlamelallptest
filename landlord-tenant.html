Landlord / Tenant

Practical guidance when a rental dispute becomes a legal matter.

Landlord-tenant disputes often involve both legal requirements and immediate practical concerns. Rent, possession of the property, notices, lease terms and court deadlines can all become important at the same time. Cohen & Lamela assists clients with landlord-tenant matters, including eviction-related proceedings and disputes arising from residential rental relationships.

The firm reviews the lease, notices, payment history, correspondence and other relevant documents to understand what has happened and what procedural steps may be available. Because landlord-tenant matters can be document-driven, careful preparation before a court appearance is especially important.

Landlord-tenant matters may involve

  • Eviction-related proceedings
  • Nonpayment and lease-related disputes
  • Notices affecting possession or tenancy
  • Questions concerning lease terms and obligations
  • Disputes that require landlord-tenant court appearances
  • Pre-litigation review of documents and communications

How the firm can help

  • Review leases, notices, payment records and written communications
  • Identify procedural steps and documentation relevant to the dispute
  • Explain the court process and the client's practical options
  • Prepare for landlord-tenant proceedings
  • Represent clients in court when appropriate

Documents matter

Bring the complete lease and any renewals or amendments, not only the page that appears most relevant. Also gather notices, rent ledgers or payment records, emails or text messages concerning the dispute, prior court paperwork and any other written communication between the parties. A complete record can help the attorney evaluate the matter more efficiently.

What to expect when you call

The attorney will want to know whether a court case has already been filed, whether any notices have been served, what the lease provides and whether there are upcoming deadlines. The initial review can help determine which documents or procedural issues require immediate attention.

1Review the Tenancy

If retained, the firm provides guidance and representation as the matter proceeds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents are important in a landlord-tenant case?

The complete lease, amendments, notices, payment records, correspondence and existing court paperwork are often important. Bringing the full record helps the attorney understand the dispute.

Can the firm assist with eviction-related matters?

Yes. The firm handles landlord-tenant matters, including eviction-related proceedings and disputes involving possession, notices and lease obligations.

What if a court date has already been scheduled?

Contact the firm promptly and provide the court notice. Upcoming hearing dates and filing deadlines can affect what preparation is needed.

Should I bring text messages or emails between the parties?

Yes. Written communications may be relevant depending on the dispute. Keep them organized and bring copies or screenshots if available.
