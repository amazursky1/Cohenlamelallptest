Municipal Law

Local guidance for traffic, ordinance, and other municipal court matters.

Municipal court is where many New Jersey residents encounter the legal system for the first time. Although the courtroom may be local, the consequences of a municipal charge can still be meaningful. Cohen & Lamela represents clients in municipal matters and brings substantial familiarity with the procedures, participants and practical realities of New Jersey municipal courts.

Jeffery M. Cohen's background includes service as a former Municipal Prosecutor for the Township of Union and later as Union Township Public Defender. That experience provides the firm with perspective from multiple sides of the municipal court process.

Municipal matters may include

  • Traffic violations and motor-vehicle summonses
  • DUI and DWI matters
  • Local ordinance complaints
  • Disorderly persons and other municipal-level charges
  • License-related issues connected to traffic or court matters
  • Multiple summonses arising from the same incident

How the firm can help

  • Review summonses, complaints and related court notices
  • Explain the nature of the charge and the municipal court process
  • Identify factual and legal issues that may affect the matter
  • Appear with clients in municipal court and communicate with the appropriate parties
  • Prepare the matter for hearing or trial when necessary

Experience with the local process

Understanding municipal practice is not simply a matter of knowing where the courthouse is. Each case requires attention to the allegations, the available evidence, the governing law and the procedures that determine how the case moves forward. The firm's long history in municipal practice helps clients approach the process with a clearer understanding of what to expect.

What to expect when you call

Have the summons, complaint or notice available and be ready to provide the municipality, court date and a brief explanation of what happened. If the matter arose from a traffic stop or incident involving several charges, provide all related paperwork so the attorney can review the matter as a whole.

Frequently Asked Questions

What kinds of cases are handled in municipal court?

Municipal courts commonly handle traffic matters, DUI or DWI cases, local ordinance complaints, disorderly persons charges and other matters assigned to municipal jurisdiction.

Do I need to appear in court personally?

That depends on the charge, the court and the circumstances. The attorney can review the notice and explain whether your personal appearance is required.

What documents should I bring?

Bring the summons, complaint, court notice and any paperwork related to the incident. If several charges arose from the same event, provide all of them.

Why does municipal court experience matter?

Municipal matters have their own procedures and practical considerations. Familiarity with those procedures can help clients understand what to expect and prepare appropriately.
