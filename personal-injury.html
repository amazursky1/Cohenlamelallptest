Personal Injury

Guidance after an injury changes your day-to-day life.

An unexpected injury can create questions about medical care, insurance coverage, time away from work and who may be responsible for what happened. Cohen & Lamela represents individuals and families in personal injury matters and helps clients understand the legal process while they focus on recovery.

The firm begins by looking closely at how the incident occurred, the available documentation and the effect the injury has had on the client. From there, the attorneys can explain the potential claim process, communication with insurance companies and the steps that may be necessary if a matter cannot be resolved through negotiation.

Personal injury matters may involve

  • Injuries arising from automobile and other motor-vehicle accidents
  • Slip-and-fall and premises-related incidents
  • Claims involving negligent conduct or unsafe conditions
  • Insurance disputes connected to an injury claim
  • Serious injuries that result in medical treatment, lost time or ongoing limitations

How the firm can help

A personal injury claim is often more than a single form or insurance conversation. The strength of a matter may depend on the facts of the incident, available records, medical documentation, insurance information and the ability to show how the injury affected the client.

  • Evaluate the circumstances surrounding the injury and identify important factual issues
  • Review accident reports, photographs, medical information and insurance correspondence
  • Explain the claims process and important procedural considerations
  • Communicate and negotiate on the client's behalf where appropriate
  • Prepare the matter for litigation when a negotiated resolution is not appropriate or available

What should you bring to the first conversation?

If available, bring accident or incident reports, photographs, insurance information, medical records or discharge paperwork, correspondence from insurers, witness information and any documents showing time missed from work. Do not worry if you do not have everything yet; the initial discussion can help identify what additional information may be useful.

What to expect when you call

The first conversation is designed to understand what happened, when it happened, the injuries involved and what has occurred since. The attorney can identify immediate concerns, explain what additional information may be needed and discuss potential next steps based on the facts of the matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I speak with an attorney after an injury?

It is generally helpful to speak with counsel early enough to preserve documents, understand insurance communications and identify any deadlines that may apply. The appropriate timing depends on the facts of the incident.

What documents are useful for a personal injury consultation?

Police or incident reports, photographs, insurance information, medical records, discharge paperwork, witness information and correspondence from insurers can all be useful if available.

Will every personal injury claim go to court?

No. Some matters may be resolved through negotiation, while others may require litigation. The path depends on the facts, the available evidence and how the parties respond to the claim.

What if I am still receiving medical treatment?

Ongoing treatment is common after an injury. The attorney can review the current status of the matter and explain what additional medical documentation may become relevant as the claim progresses.
