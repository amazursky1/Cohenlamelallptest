Traffic Law

Representation for traffic, license and municipal-court matters.

A traffic summons may appear routine, but the consequences can extend beyond the amount printed on a ticket. Depending on the charge and the client's driving history, a matter may raise concerns about points, driving privileges, insurance consequences or multiple related violations. Cohen & Lamela represents clients in New Jersey municipal courts and helps them understand what the summons alleges before deciding how to proceed.

The firm brings extensive municipal-court experience to traffic matters, including Jeffery M. Cohen's prior service as Union Township Municipal Prosecutor and Public Defender. That background provides practical familiarity with how municipal cases move through the court system.

Traffic matters may include

  • Moving violations and motor-vehicle summonses
  • Speeding and other roadway-related charges
  • License-related matters
  • Multiple tickets issued during the same traffic stop
  • Traffic matters connected to an accident
  • Municipal court appearances involving disputed violations

How the firm can help

  • Review the summons and the circumstances of the stop
  • Explain the municipal court process and issues that may affect the case
  • Evaluate available documentation, reports or other evidence
  • Discuss the practical options available before the scheduled court date
  • Represent the client in municipal court when appropriate

Do not look at only one ticket

If you received several summonses at the same time, provide all of them to the attorney. Related charges may need to be considered together. Also have your court notice, accident information if applicable and any other documents connected with the stop or incident available for review.

What to expect when you call

The first discussion will focus on the charge, the municipality, the scheduled court date and what occurred during the stop or incident. The attorney can explain what information may be relevant and whether there are additional issues that should be considered before the court appearance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I bring every traffic ticket I received?

Yes. If several summonses were issued during the same stop, they should be reviewed together because the charges may involve related facts or consequences.

Do I have to appear in municipal court?

That depends on the charge and the court notice. The attorney can review the summons and explain what appearance requirements apply.

Can traffic charges affect my driver's license?

Certain charges can carry licensing consequences. The specific effect depends on the violation, the driver's history and the outcome of the case.

What if the ticket arose from an accident?

Bring the summonses along with any accident report or related documentation. The traffic case and any separate accident claim may involve different legal issues.
